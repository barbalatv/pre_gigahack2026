## 0. Canon Rules & Epistemic Standards

This document establishes an uncompromised, adversarial standard of truth. Every statement is classified into one of five evidentiary categories:

1. **`VERIFIED FACT`**: A statement backed directly by primary authoritative documentation (official websites, ticket operators, governmental press releases, official published agendas, official event organizers' reports).
2. **`VERIFIED OBSERVED PRACTICE`**: An empirical fact regarding what participants actually executed, what repositories contain, or what organizers accepted in practice, decoupled from whether an official rule explicitly authorized it.
3. **`STRONG INFERENCE`**: A conclusion with high logical probability derived from multiple concurring verified facts and practices, where direct statutory text is absent but the operational reality is clear.
4. **`WEAK INFERENCE`**: A plausible operational hypothesis based on partial or indirect indicators, requiring explicit verification before critical reliance.
5. **`CONFLICTING EVIDENCE`**: A situation where two or more credible primary or secondary sources make mutually irreconcilable statements regarding the same parameter. Both sides must be documented without picking a winner.
6. **`UNKNOWN`**: A domain where no public evidence, rule, or verifiable empirical record exists.

### Fundamental Operating Axioms:
* **Absence of a public prohibition does not equal official permission (`Absence of Enforcement != Permission`).** The fact that an action was not detected or penalized in the past does not mean it is an authorized entitlement under hackathon regulations.
* **Observed participant behavior does not automatically establish official policy.** If past winners committed code in bulk 20 minutes before a deadline, it proves only that their submission was accepted, not that the rules formally waived originality or continuous version control.
* **Current 2026 official sources strictly outrank historical analogy.** If an official 2026 publication contradicts an observation from 2023 or 2024, the 2026 official document is authoritative.

*Mathematical Weights Disclaimer:* All percentage distributions (e.g., 25% Innovation, 20% Feasibility) previously asserted were analytical artifacts and **are purged from this Canon**. Scoring rubrics are qualitative or integer-based (e.g., 1–5 or 1–10).
